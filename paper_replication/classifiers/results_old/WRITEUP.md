### SAE-vs-raw deltas

| dataset | hook | raw | sae | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rf3_toxinpred3 | block12 | 0.832 | 0.860 | +0.028 ↑ SAE |
| rf3_toxinpred3 | block16 | 0.846 | 0.834 | −0.013 ↓ raw |
| rfd3_safeprotein | block6 | 0.672 | 0.712 | +0.040 ↑ SAE |
| rfd3_safeprotein | block8 | 0.781 | 0.760 | −0.021 ↓ raw |
| rfd3_safeprotein | block12 | 0.851 | 0.791 | −0.060 ↓ raw |

Splits 2-3 in either direction; the deltas are mostly within the ±0.06 noise floor on RFD3. **Don't claim "raw is better than SAE" or vice-versa from this alone.**

### Observations

1. **Deeper hooks generalize better.** RFD3 raw: 0.67 → 0.78 → 0.85 from block6 → block8 → block12. Late layers carry the toxicity-relevant signal more linearly.
2. **SAE encoding regularizes.** Train/eval gap is consistently smaller for `sae_encode` (mean gap 0.119) vs `identity` (mean gap 0.190). For block6 the gap drops from 0.323 to 0.172.
3. **Heavy overfitting everywhere.** Even after SAE regularization, train AUROC ≥ 0.88 vs eval ≤ 0.86. With 220 RFD3 train designs and 768-12288 features, the LR is memorizing.
4. **Asymmetric feature selection caveat.** The fit configs apply `select_top_k=50` only to `sae_encode` (line 108 in `generate_configs.py`), not `identity`. So SAE LRs train on 50 features while raw LRs train on 768. SAE wins partly from this regularization, not pure representational quality.

### Why SAE > raw on block6 specifically

Three reasons:
1. **Implicit regularization** — sparser pooled features after Top-K, plus the `select_top_k=50` advantage above.
2. **Early-layer features are less linearly separable in raw form** — block6 encodes general structural primitives. The SAE was trained to factorize these into individually meaningful directions; some of them happen to align with toxicity, while no single raw channel does.
3. **Bias-variance tradeoff** — with 220 designs, fitting 50 sparse SAE coefficients is more data-efficient than 768 dense raw ones.

## Sanity checks performed

### 1. Length-only baseline
Logistic regression on sequence length alone — confirms the AUROCs above are not driven by trivial length differences.

| dataset | length range (pos / neg) | length-only test AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| rfd3_safeprotein | mean 218 [45–580] / mean 186 [11–633] | 0.554 |
| rf3_toxinpred3 | mean 27.4 [19–35] / mean 27.5 [19–35] | 0.532 |

ToxinPred3 is essentially length-controlled by design. RFD3 SafeProtein hazards are slightly longer on average but with massive overlap; length alone barely beats chance.

### 2. Per-length-bin breakdown (RFD3 block12 identity probe)

| length bin | n_test | pos | neg | bin AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [100, 200) | 16 | 9 | 7 | 0.921 |
| [200, 1000) | 37 | 22 | 15 | 0.829 |

Consistent across length range; the headline 0.85 isn't a single bin's AUROC. (The [0,100) bin had only 2 designs — insufficient class balance.)

### 3. SAE health (`sae_health_check.py`)
All five trained SAEs are healthy — l0 ≈ 80 (matches k), `frac_alive ≥ 0.94`, FVE ≥ 96%, cossim ≥ 98%. The "1% feature utilization" we initially saw was a pipeline bug (pool-then-encode instead of encode-then-pool); the fix patched `detectors/pipelines/cache.py` and now 81%+ of dictionary features fire across designs.

## Important caveats

1. **Small RFD3 test set (n=55).** AUROC 90% CI ≈ ±0.06. Gaps under 0.06 are within noise.
2. **No homology-clustered split.** Paralogs from training may have leaked into test, inflating both numbers uniformly. mmseqs2 easy-cluster at 30% identity is the standard fix; not yet done.
3. **Single seed.** No error bars from re-running with different SAE inits or probe seeds.
4. **Asymmetric feature selection.** `select_top_k=50` applied to SAE only — biases SAE-vs-raw comparisons in favor of SAE.
5. **Cross-model comparison still on different label sets.** RFD3 was evaluated on SafeProtein/UniProt; RF3 on ToxinPred3. The third cell (RF3 on SafeProtein/UniProt, with the new SAE checkpoint at `outputs/sae/2026-04-26_22-03-07/train`) is now configured in `generate_configs.py` but labels aren't built yet.
6. **2D-per-step hooks skipped.** `token_initializer_outputs` and `to_si` are 3D in the h5; `pool_design` requires 4D. A one-line `expand_dims` patch in `cache._iter_design_activations` would unlock them.
7. **No external category breakdown.** SafeProtein doesn't ship toxin-family labels (e.g. PFAM/InterPro). A by-family AUROC breakdown would tell us whether the classifier generalizes across toxin families or just recognizes one dominant subset — needs an external annotation step.

## Recommended next steps (priority order)

1. **Run the third cell (RF3 on SafeProtein/UniProt).** Build labels for `rf3_safeprotein`, regenerate configs, run the sweep. This is the only matched RFD3-vs-RF3 comparison.
2. **Symmetrize feature selection.** Add `select_top_k: 50` to identity fit configs (or remove it from sae_encode). Re-sweep, compare apples to apples.
3. **Homology-clustered split.** mmseqs2 easy-cluster at 30% identity, hold out whole clusters. Often drops AUROC 0.10–0.20; the *real* generalization number.
4. **Multi-seed.** 3 SAE seeds × 5 probe folds → mean ± std on every cell. Single biggest credibility upgrade for any reported gap.
5. **Annotate hazards by family** (PFAM/InterPro), report per-family AUROC. If one family dominates, the headline is misleading.
6. **Patch the 3D-hook crash** (~2 lines in `_iter_design_activations`) to enable `token_initializer_outputs` and `to_si` cells.
